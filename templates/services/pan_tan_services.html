Know the difference

What Are PAN & TAN?

PAN is a ten-character Permanent Account Number issued by the Income Tax Department. It identifies individuals and entities in tax, banking and specified financial transactions.

TAN is a separate ten-character Tax Deduction and Collection Account Number. It is required for persons or organisations that deduct tax at source (TDS) or collect tax at source (TCS). It must be quoted in applicable TDS/TCS returns, challans, certificates and communications.

Many businesses need PAN for their financial identity, and may also need TAN when they begin deducting or collecting tax.

At a glance

PAN vs TAN

PAN

  • Purpose: Taxpayer and financial identification
  • Who requires it: Individuals and eligible entities
  • Issued by: Income Tax Department
  • Format: Ten-character alphanumeric number
  • Used for: Tax, banking and specified transactions
  • Mandatory: Where prescribed under tax rules
  • Example: ABCDE1234F

TAN

  • Purpose: TDS and TCS compliance
  • Who requires it: Deductors and collectors of tax
  • Issued by: Income Tax Department
  • Format: Ten-character alphanumeric number
  • Used for: TDS/TCS returns, challans and certificates
  • Mandatory: For persons required to deduct or collect tax
  • Example: MUMN12345A

Many businesses require both PAN and TAN depending on their nature of operations.
